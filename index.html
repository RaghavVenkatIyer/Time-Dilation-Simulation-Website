Formulas used
same as the script

    

      
Schwarzschild radiusr_s = 2GM / c²

      
Time dilationt_faller = t_viewer · √(1 − r_s/r)

      
Escape velocityv_esc = √(2GM / r)

      
Gravitational accelerationg = GM / r²

      
Orbital velocityv_orb = √(GM / r)

      
Gravitational redshiftz = 1/√(1 − r_s/r) − 1

      
Compressed lengthL_faller = L_viewer · √(1 − r_s/r)

      
Gravitational potential energyU = −GmM / r

      
Hover accelerationa = GM/r² · √(1 − r_s/r)As written in the script. Textbook GR divides by √(1 − r_s/r) instead, which grows without limit near the horizon.

      
Orbital periodT = 2π √(r³ / GM)

      
Tidal accelerationΔa = 2GMh / r³

    

  


  

    
About the picture
what is measured, what is exact, what is drawn for clarity

    

      

        
Modelled on Sagittarius A*

        
The default is the black hole at the centre of the Milky Way: 4.297 million solar masses, about 8,277 parsecs (27,000 light-years) from Earth, from the GRAVITY Collaboration's star-orbit measurements.

        
At the default 4 rs the probe needs about 50 minutes per lap at roughly a third of light speed. That is close to the hot spots GRAVITY tracked in 2018 looping around the hole during infrared flares. Earth's view tilts the camera to the nearly face-on angle those loops suggest, which also fits the Event Horizon Telescope image.

      

      

        
Physically exact

        
Each pixel follows one light ray through the Schwarzschild geometry. The dark shadow of radius 2.6 rs, the thin photon ring, the far side of the disk arching over the top and the stars smeared into arcs all come out of that calculation. Switch Light bending off to compare with flat space.

        
Disk brightness and colour include Doppler beaming and gravitational redshift, so the side rotating toward you is brighter and bluer. The grid is Flamm's paraboloid, the true curved shape of space around a non-rotating black hole.

      

      

        
Drawn for clarity

        
The astronaut is enlarged millions of times and hovers on rockets, which is the situation the time dilation and hover formulas describe. They redden with gravitational redshift and stretch with the tidal force.

        
The real gas around Sagittarius A* is a faint, puffy flow, shown here as a bright thin disk. The hole does not spin, matching the script's formulas. The grid hangs below the hole to keep the view clear, and distances beyond 12 rs are compressed.

      

    

  


  
Newtonian formulas (escape velocity, orbital velocity and period, tidal force) are exactly as in the original script. Real orbits are only stable outside 3 rs, and light can circle the hole at 1.5 rs; the picture draws the disk from 3 rs outward for that reason, and the orbiting probe disappears inside 1.5 rs.
